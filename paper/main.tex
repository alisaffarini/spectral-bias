\begin{abstract}
Neural metric learning trains an embedding network with a triplet or
contrastive loss, but which similarity geometries gradient descent can
actually fit on a finite training horizon is poorly understood. We
address this by bridging two theoretical lenses --- the neural tangent
kernel (NTK) for wide-network gradient dynamics, and the
Burer--Monteiro factorisation for low-rank semidefinite optimization
--- in a way that has not been combined for primal feature-space
training: gradient flow on a wide embedding network induces a closed-form
\emph{kernel-preconditioned matrix flow} on the Gram matrix of the
embeddings, with the NTK at initialization as the preconditioner. The
resulting dynamics exhibit a \emph{quadratic} spectral implicit bias
--- per-mode displacement of the Gram matrix scales as the square of
the corresponding NTK eigenvalue, distinct from the linear-in-eigenvalue
rates known for scalar NTK regression --- which pins down the effective
hypothesis class on bounded training horizons: target structures aligned
with the kernel's top eigenspace are reachable, while those concentrated
in its bottom eigenspace remain near initialization. We verify the
slope-$\leq 2$ bound across synthetic data, Fashion-MNIST,
frozen-feature CIFAR-10, and an end-to-end CNN trained from scratch.
The same analysis prescribes a one-line algorithmic correction ---
applying the inverse kernel to the embedding-side gradient --- which
improves hard-negative retrieval but not easy retrieval, exactly the
regime where the theory predicts an effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern retrieval, re-identification, contrastive pre-training, and
representation-learning pipelines all depend on a metric-learning
objective trained with stochastic gradient descent --- yet the
\emph{shape} of the geometry such an objective induces, and in
particular which directions in feature space are reachable in finite
training, has no closed-form characterisation. Practitioners cannot
predict in advance which similarity structures their network will fit;
when retrieval underperforms on hard negatives or fails under
adversarial perturbation, there is no theory pointing to a
principled correction. This paper provides one.

Neural metric learning trains an embedding $f_\theta: \R^d \to \R^k$ so that
$\norm{f_\theta(x) - f_\theta(y)}^2$ encodes a desired similarity geometry.
The standard recipe is to minimize a triplet or contrastive loss --- a
twice-differentiable function of the embeddings' pairwise inner products
--- so that the loss depends on $\theta$ only through the Gram matrix
$G = FF^\top$ of the embeddings. This Gram-matrix view is what makes
metric learning amenable to two distinct theoretical frameworks that
otherwise bear on it from very different angles.
The \emph{neural tangent kernel} \citep{jacot2018neural,lee2019wide} describes
sufficiently wide networks as kernel methods governed by a fixed kernel
$\Theta$, and yields linear ODEs for the network's predictions under
gradient flow. The \emph{Burer--Monteiro factorization}
\citep{burer2003nonlinear,burer2005local}, central to low-rank semidefinite
programming, parameterizes a positive semidefinite matrix $X$ as
$X = UU^\top$ and uses $U$ as the optimization variable; the resulting
gradient flow on $X$ takes the form $\dot{X} = -2(EX + XE)$.

The two frameworks are usually treated in isolation. We connect them by
observing that an embedding network with twice-differentiable loss on
$G = FF^\top$ is itself a Burer--Monteiro factorization, but in the
non-linear feature space induced by the network rather than the linear
parameterization $G = UU^\top$ of classical low-rank SDP. The NTK enters
this picture as an explicit \emph{kernel preconditioner} on the BM
dynamics. Concretely, in the NTK regime the network's Gram matrix
evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kbm-intro}
\dot{G} \;=\; -2\,\bigl(K\,\E\,G + G\,\E\,K\bigr),
\qquad \E = \partial L / \partial G,
\end{equation}
which reduces to the classical BM flow when $K = I$. We call \eqref{eq:kbm-intro}
the \emph{kernel-preconditioned Burer--Monteiro} (kBM) flow.

The equivalence is itself a conceptual bridge, but the load-bearing use
of it is to extract a \emph{spectral implicit bias} that does not follow
from the NTK ODE alone: in $K$'s eigenbasis, mode-$(i,j)$ evolution is
multiplied by $\lambda_i + \lambda_j$, and at NTK initialization per-mode
displacement scales \emph{quadratically} in $\lambda_i$. The trajectory
of $G(t)$ is therefore confined to $K$'s top eigenspaces on bounded
training horizons, regardless of where the loss would otherwise push it.
We verify the slope-$\leq 2$ bound across synthetic data, Fashion-MNIST,
CIFAR-10, an end-to-end CNN, and a ViT empirical NTK --- including in
the finite-width CNN and ViT settings where the NTK approximation is
only nominal --- and derive a one-line $K^{-1}$ correction whose
regime-of-effect prediction (gain on hard negatives, tie on easy
retrieval) the data confirm.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) \emph{kBM equivalence} (Theorems~\ref{thm:kbm}--\ref{thm:depth}):
single-layer and depth-$L$ ReLU gradient flow on $L(G)$ obeys
$\dot G = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$, recovering classical BM when $K=I$;
(ii) \emph{spectral implicit-bias theorems}: a model-free linear-in-$\lambda$
rate ordering (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}) sharpens to a quadratic
prediction in the NTK regime (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined});
(iii) \emph{multi-seed numerical verification} on synthetic data,
Fashion-MNIST, frozen-feature CIFAR-10, an end-to-end CNN, and a
ViT empirical NTK (Appendix~\ref{app:vit}), with slopes spanning
$1.14$--$2.19$ (all~$\leq 2$);
(iv) \emph{theory-derived correction}: a one-line $K^{-1}$ preconditioner
flattens the slope across all settings and improves hard-negative
retrieval with monotone-in-$K$ gains; on CIFAR-10 at
$N_{\mathrm{train}}\!=\!4000$ ($10$ seeds) it adds $+1.82$/$+2.10$/$+2.17$~pp
to R@$5$/$10$/$20$ (Bonferroni $p\leq 0.004$ on each) while \emph{tying}
on easy R@$10$/R@$20$ --- exactly the regime of effect theory predicts.
The effect attenuates on high-class-count benchmarks where
bottom-eigenspace gradient signal is dominated by sampling noise
(Appendices~\ref{app:scaled},~\ref{app:multidataset});
(v) \emph{practical-robustness corroboration}: the same bottom-eigenmode
reach widens the embedding-space PGD margin --- across three
$\ell_\infty$ attack budgets $\varepsilon\!\in\!\{4,8,16\}/255$
($5$ seeds each), $K^{-1}$ retains
$4$--$8\times$ adversarial R@$\{5,10,20\}$ relative to vanilla, with
the margin advantage \emph{not degrading} as $\varepsilon$ grows
(Appendix~\ref{app:adv});
(vi) the bias is empirically \emph{distinct from Razin--Cohen rank bias}.
All results assume the standard isotropic NTK approximation; we do not
claim a quantitative generalisation bound (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{NTK theory.}
The NTK framework \citep{jacot2018neural} reduces wide-network training
to kernel regression; \citet{lee2019wide} extend the convergence to any
depth $L$ and give the standard finite-width concentration rate.
\citet{chizat2020implicit} formalises the lazy/rich dichotomy. The
\emph{Tensor Programs} framework \citep{yang2020tensor} establishes
the joint NTK factorisation across architecture classes (FCN, CNN,
attention) at infinite width that we leverage in the depth-$L$ proof
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{Burer--Monteiro factorization, and the closest BM-meets-NN prior art.}
\citet{burer2003nonlinear,burer2005local} introduced $X=UU^\top$ for SDPs;
\citet{boumal2016non,bhojanapalli2016global,ge2016matrix,park2017non}
characterise the resulting non-convex landscape.
\citet{sahiner2022scaling} apply BM to the \emph{dual} variable of the
Pilanci--Ergen convex reformulation of two-layer networks for
computational scalability. Our connection is distinct: we identify that
\emph{primal} gradient flow on a wide feature network induces a
kernel-preconditioned BM flow on the embeddings' Gram matrix
$G = FF^\top$, with the NTK as the explicit preconditioner. To our
knowledge no prior work derives this primal-Gram kBM equivalence for
metric learning or extracts a spectral implicit bias from it.

\paragraph{Implicit bias.}
\citet{gunasekar2017implicit,arora2019implicit,razin2020implicit}
characterise an implicit \emph{rank} bias in matrix and tensor
factorisation. \citet{soudry2018implicit,lyu2019gradient} characterise
asymptotic margin direction. Our bias is in the spectrum of the NTK,
not the rank of $G$ nor the parameter direction; we demonstrate the
dissociation in Section~\ref{sec:exp-rank-vs-spec}.

\paragraph{NTK eigenvalue dynamics and spectral bias.}
\citet{bordelon2020spectrum,canatar2021spectral} give mode-wise
generalisation error for NTK \emph{kernel regression} on scalar
targets, with rates linear in the kernel eigenvalue.
\citet{rahaman2019spectral} study Fourier-frequency bias and
\citet{basri2020frequency} analyse NTK sphere-harmonic decay --- the
latter sharing our eigenbasis but, again, for the scalar predictor
with linear-in-$\lambda$ rate. Our object is the \emph{matrix-valued}
Gram $G$ under triplet loss, and the per-mode bound is
\emph{quadratic} in $\lambda$: the extra factor of $\lambda$ comes
from Funk--Hecke alignment of $G_0 = F_0 F_0^\top$ with $K$ at NTK
initialization (Proposition~\ref{prop:align}) --- a property that
exists only because the embedding Gram inherits $K$'s spectrum at
init, which has no scalar analogue. Recent dynamical mean-field
analyses of feature learning beyond the lazy NTK regime
\citep{bordelon2023self,bordelon2024dynamical} characterise how the
NTK \emph{itself} drifts during training; our slope bound is derived
in the lazy regime where the NTK is fixed, isolating the bias
contributed by the loss + factorisation.

\paragraph{Kernel-spectrum-modifying preconditioners.}
\citet{bach2017breaking} establishes the canonical eigenvalue-decay
calibration of arc-cosine NTK kernels on the sphere that grounds our
slope analysis (Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral}). The closest
algorithmic neighbour is \citet{amir2023controlling}, who reweight
input features to install a prescribed NTK spectrum and thereby
control the function-space inductive bias of a wide \emph{scalar}
predictor; their analysis runs through the function-space NTK ODE on
the residual $\hat f - y$, with no matrix-valued object in scope.
Our intervention is structurally distinct on three axes: (i)
\emph{object} --- we precondition $\partial L/\partial F$ in a triplet
flow on the matrix-valued Gram $G = FF^\top$, not a scalar predictor;
(ii) \emph{derivation} --- the $K^{-1}$ correction is the canonical
cancellation of the kBM preconditioner in
$\dot G = -2(KEG + GEK)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:kbm}), which has no
analogue in their function-space setting; (iii) \emph{regime of
effect} --- we predict and falsify a hard-vs-easy retrieval asymmetry
tied to $K$'s bottom-eigenspace reach (Section~\ref{sec:exp-cifar}),
a regime their analysis does not address.
ScaledGD~\citep{tong2021accelerating} and the over-parameterised
preconditioned-GD line~\citep{xu2023power} use a factor-side
preconditioner $(U^\top U)^{-1}$ to remove condition-number dependence
in low-rank \emph{matrix sensing}; our $K^{-1}$ acts on the
\emph{NTK feature Gram} of a non-linear network with triplet loss, a
qualitatively different operation tied to the kBM equivalence rather
than to ill-conditioning of the iterates. Beyond the conceptual
distinction, we sweep the natural one-parameter family of
kernel-spectrum-modifying preconditioners $K^{-\alpha}$ for
$\alpha \in \{0, \tfrac{1}{2}, 1, 2\}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha}):
the empirical R@$10$ gain is non-monotone in $\alpha$ with $\alpha\!=\!1$
uniquely optimal --- exactly the value the kBM derivation predicts.
This rules out a generic ``any kernel-spectrum modification helps''
explanation: the data select $K^{-1}$ specifically, not a nearby
alternative. To our knowledge no prior work derives the primal-Gram
kBM equivalence or characterises the slope-$\leq 2$ bound for
matrix-valued NTK dynamics.

\paragraph{Metric-learning empirics.}
\citet{schroff2015facenet,weinberger2009distance,movshovitz2017no,wang2017deep}
develop triplet metric learning empirically; our convex-reformulation
neighbour is \citet{pilanci2020neural}.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Setup}

Let $X \in \R^{N \times d}$ be a data matrix with rows $x_i$. We denote by
$F = f_\theta(X) \in \R^{N \times M}$ the embeddings produced by a depth-$L$
fully-connected ReLU network of hidden width $M$ with parameters
$\theta$; we use the last hidden-layer activations as the embedding so
the embedding dimension equals the hidden width. The corresponding Gram
matrix is $G = FF^\top \in \R^{N \times N}$. We
consider any twice-differentiable loss $L : \R^{N \times N} \to \R$ on $G$;
triplet loss is the running example. Write $\E := \partial L / \partial G$.

We use the standard NTK parameterization with weights initialized
$\mathcal{N}(0, 1/d_{\text{in}})$ at each layer and no output normalization;
under this convention $G_{ij}$ is $\Theta(1)$ at initialization and our
expressions are scale-free.

\subsection{Single-layer kBM equivalence}

\begin{definition}[NTK at the data]
The Jacobian of the embedding is $J(x; \theta) = \nabla_\theta f_\theta(x)$.
The (output-summed) NTK at $x_i, x_j$ is
$\kappa(x_i, x_j) := \tr\!\bigl(J(x_i)J(x_j)^\top\bigr)/M$.
We write $K_{ij} := \kappa(x_i, x_j)$ for the empirical kernel matrix.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[kBM equivalence, single layer]
\label{thm:kbm}
For a single-layer ReLU network $F_{i\cdot} = \mathrm{ReLU}(x_i^\top W)$
with $W \in \R^{d \times M}$ trained by gradient flow on $L(G)$, the Gram
matrix $G$ evolves, in the limit $M \to \infty$, as
\[
\dot{G} \;=\; -2\,\bigl(K\,\E\,G + G\,\E\,K\bigr).
\]
For finite width $M$ the same equation holds up to an
$O_p(M^{-1/2})$ correction in the right-hand side: both
$\norm{K^{(M)} - K}_{op}$ and the residual in the Gram-Gram NTK
factorisation are $O_p(M^{-1/2})$ under standard assumptions
\citep{jacot2018neural,lee2019wide,yang2020tensor}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under gradient flow the Gram-Gram NTK
$\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} := \langle \nabla_\theta G_{ij}, \nabla_\theta G_{kl}\rangle$
admits a four-term contraction
$\kappa_{ik}G_{jl} + \kappa_{il}G_{jk} + \kappa_{jk}G_{il} + \kappa_{jl}G_{ik}$
in the $M\to\infty$ limit. Substituting into
$\dot G_{ij} = -\sum_{kl}\E_{kl}\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)}$ and using
$\E=\E^\top$ collapses the four terms into $KEG+GEK$. Full derivation:
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
When $K = I$, Theorem~\ref{thm:kbm} reduces to the classical
Burer--Monteiro flow $\dot G = -2(\E G + G \E)$ \citep{burer2003nonlinear},
verifying that the classical BM dynamics are a special case.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Depth-$L$ extension}

The depth-$L$ NTK admits the recursive form of \citet{jacot2018neural}:
$\Theta^{(\ell)} = \Sigma^{(\ell)} + \dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)} \odot \Theta^{(\ell-1)}$
with $\Theta^{(1)} = \Sigma^{(1)} + \dot{\Sigma}^{(1)} \odot \langle x,y\rangle$,
where $\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ is the layer-$\ell$ activation kernel (the
Cho--Saul arc-cosine kernel) and $\dot\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ its derivative
\citep{cho2009kernel}.

\begin{theorem}[kBM equivalence, depth $L$]
\label{thm:depth}
For a depth-$L$ fully-connected ReLU network in NTK parameterization, the
Gram matrix $G$ evolves under gradient flow as $\dot G = -2(K_L \E G + G \E K_L)$
where $K_L$ is the depth-$L$ NTK from the Jacot recursion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$G = FF^\top$ holds at every depth, so the Gram-derivative identity
$\dot G_{ij} = -\sum_{kl}\E_{kl}\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)}$ does not depend on
$L$. Tensor-Programs II
\citep{yang2020tensor} gives the joint factorisation: the Gram-Gram
NTK $\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)}$ at infinite width is
$\kappa_L(x_i,x_k) G_{jl} + \kappa_L(x_i,x_l) G_{jk} + \kappa_L(x_j,x_k) G_{il} + \kappa_L(x_j,x_l) G_{ik}$,
where the read-out cross terms ($m\!\neq\!n$) cancel because last-layer
weights $a_m, a_n$ are i.i.d.\ with $\mathbb{E}[a_m a_n]\!=\!0$ and
factor out of the joint Gaussian governing the pre-activations. The
four-term contraction collapses to $K_L \E G + G \E K_L$ by symmetry
of $\E$ and $G$, exactly as in Thm~\ref{thm:kbm}; the only depth
dependence is replacing $\kappa_1$ with the recursive $\kappa_L$.
Full derivation: Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral implicit bias}

The kBM flow takes a particularly clean form in $K$'s eigenbasis, and this
form yields the paper's central new result.

\begin{lemma}[kBM in $K$'s eigenbasis]
\label{lem:eigen}
Let $K = U\Lambda U^\top$ with $\Lambda = \diag(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_N)$,
$\lambda_1 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_N \geq 0$. Define
$\Gtilde := U^\top G U$ and $\Etilde := U^\top \E U$, and assume $\E$ is
symmetric (true whenever $L$ is a function of pairwise inner products of
$F$, including triplet, contrastive, and pairwise losses). Then the kBM
flow becomes
\[
\dot{\Gtilde} \;=\; -2\,\bigl(\Lambda\,\Etilde\,\Gtilde + \Gtilde\,\Etilde\,\Lambda\bigr),
\]
and the $(i,j)$ entry satisfies
$\dot{\Gtilde}_{ij} = -2\bigl(\lambda_i (\Etilde\Gtilde)_{ij} +
\lambda_j (\Gtilde\Etilde)_{ij}\bigr)$. By symmetry of $\Etilde$ and
$\Gtilde$, the diagonal mode satisfies
$\dot{\Gtilde}_{ii} = -4\lambda_i (\Etilde\Gtilde)_{ii}$, so the
$i$th eigendirection of $G$ relaxes at instantaneous rate proportional to
$\lambda_i$. In particular modes with $\lambda_i = 0$ are stationary,
and modes with small $\lambda_i$ are correspondingly slow.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution of $K = U\Lambda U^\top$ and conjugation by $U^\top, U$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral implicit bias, model-free linear bound]
\label{thm:spectral}
Let $G(t)$ solve the kBM ODE $\dot G = -2(K\E G + G \E K)$ with
$L \in C^2$. Suppose $\lambda_i = 0$ for some index $i$. Then
$\Gtilde_{ij}(t) = \Gtilde_{ij}(0)$ for all $t$ and all $j$. More
generally, on any interval $[0, T]$ on which the trajectory satisfies
the stability condition
$\norm{\Etilde(s)\Gtilde(s)}_{op}, \norm{\Gtilde(s)\Etilde(s)}_{op} \leq C$
for all $s \in [0, T]$ (which holds for hinged triplet loss whenever
both $\norm{\Gtilde}_{\!op}$ and the active-triplet count remain
bounded --- the standard regime; see Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}),
every entry $(i,j)$ obeys
\[
\bigl|\Gtilde_{ij}(T) - \Gtilde_{ij}(0)\bigr|
\;\leq\; 2T(\lambda_i + \lambda_j)\,C.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Frozen-mode case: rows/columns of $\Lambda$ at index $i$ vanish in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eigen}'s expression. Bound: integrate
$|\dot{\Gtilde}_{ij}|\le 2(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)C$. Full proof:
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From a linear bound to a quadratic prediction}
\label{sec:refined}

The bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} is linear in $\lambda_i$. In our
experiments (Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral}) the empirical scaling of
diagonal displacements $|\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)|$ is consistently
\emph{quadratic} in $\lambda_i$ across depths and across both synthetic and
Fashion-MNIST data. The gap between linear bound and quadratic observation
is closed by exploiting a structural property of NTK initialization:
$G_0$ and $K$ share approximate eigenstructure, so $\Gtilde_0$ is
approximately diagonal in $K$'s eigenbasis with diagonal entries
proportional to the kernel spectrum.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate eigen-alignment at NTK init via Funk--Hecke]
\label{prop:align}
For a single-layer ReLU network in NTK parameterization with width $M$ and
inputs drawn iid from a rotationally-invariant distribution on the unit
sphere, $G_0 = F_0 F_0^\top$ converges as $M \to \infty$ to the layer-1
arc-cosine kernel $\Sigma^{(1)}$ \citep{cho2009kernel}. Both
$\Sigma^{(1)}$ and the NTK $K = \Sigma^{(1)} + \dot{\Sigma}^{(1)} \odot
\langle x_i, x_j\rangle$ are functions of the input correlation matrix
$\langle x_i, x_j\rangle$ alone; under rotational invariance they share
an asymptotic eigenbasis (the spherical harmonics on $S^{d-1}$). Assume
the empirical kernel matrix $K^{(N)}$ has spectral gap bounded below by
some $g > 0$ across its $N$ eigenvalues. Then $\Gtilde_0 := U^\top G_0 U$
is approximately diagonal, and on the empirical-rank-supported finite
range of harmonic frequencies $l \in \{0, \ldots, L_{\max}(N)\}$ the
diagonal entries satisfy $\Gtilde_0[i,i] = c_i \lambda_i$ with
$c_i \in [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$ for finite constants $c_{\min} > 0$ and
$c_{\max} \leq 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Both $\Sigma^{(1)}$ and $K$ are scalar functions of $\langle x_i,x_j\rangle$,
so Funk--Hecke \citep[Thm.~2.22]{atkinson2012spherical} diagonalises them
in the spherical-harmonic basis with eigenvalues given by Gegenbauer
integrals; the ratio $c_l\in(0,1)$ and is bounded away from zero by the
decay rates of \citet{bach2017breaking}. Empirical concentration is
$O(N^{-1/2})$ in operator norm \citep{tropp2015introduction} via
Davis--Kahan; the $O(N^{-1/2})$ \emph{Frobenius} rate on
$\Gtilde_0 - \diag(\Gtilde_0)$ used in (A1) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} follows because the perturbation
restricted to a single harmonic block has rank $O(1)$, so
$\norm{\cdot}_F \leq \sqrt{\text{block rank}}\cdot\norm{\cdot}_{op}$
and the $\sqrt{\text{block rank}}$ factor is $O(1)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Full proof: Appendix~\ref{app:prop-align}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Refined spectral implicit bias, quadratic prediction]
\label{thm:spectral-refined}
Let $G(t)$ solve the kBM ODE $\dot{G} = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$ with $L \in C^2$.
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A1)] $\Gtilde_0$ has diagonal entries
$\Gtilde_0[i,i] = c_i \lambda_i$ with $c_i \in [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$
($c_{\min}>0$ across the empirically loaded
frequency range, by the
arc-cosine-kernel decay rate of \citet{bach2017breaking}; see
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, where we also state the
finite-$N$ scope of this constant),
and the total off-diagonal mass is small in Frobenius norm:
$\norm{\Gtilde_0 - \diag(\Gtilde_0)}_F = O(N^{-1/2})$ (justified by
Proposition~\ref{prop:align});
\item[(A2)] $\Etilde(s)$ has uniformly bounded operator norm
$\norm{\Etilde(s)}_{op} \leq E_{op}$ for $s \in [0, T]$, and
$|(\Etilde(s))_{ii}| \leq E_{op}$ entrywise on the diagonal.
\end{itemize}
Then, to leading order in $T$,
\[
\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)
\;=\; -\,4\,T\,\lambda_i\,(\Etilde_0\,\Gtilde_0)_{ii} \;+\; O(T^2),
\]
and combining (A1) and (A2) via Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\boxed{\;
\bigl|\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)\bigr|
\;\leq\; 4\,c_{\max}\,T\,E_{op}\,\lambda_i^{2}
\;+\; O\!\bigl(T\,\lambda_i\,N^{-1/2}\bigr)
\;+\; O(T^2).
\;}
\]
The leading term scales as $\lambda_i^{\,2}$, matching the empirical
power-law slope $\leq 2$ observed in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral}. The bound is stated to leading order in
$T$; the $O(T^2)$ remainder is controlled by Gr\"onwall on the
quadratic-in-$\Gtilde$ ODE under (A2)
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), so the slope-$\leq 2$ envelope persists
over the integrated training horizon, not only at $T \to 0^+$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:eigen},
$\dot{\Gtilde}_{ii} = -4\lambda_i(\Etilde\Gtilde)_{ii}$. Linearising around
$t = 0$ gives
$\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0) = -4 T \lambda_i (\Etilde_0\Gtilde_0)_{ii} + O(T^2)$.
Decompose $\Gtilde_0 = D + R$ where $D = \diag(\Gtilde_0)$ and $R$ is the
off-diagonal residual. Then
$(\Etilde_0\Gtilde_0)_{ii} = (\Etilde_0)_{ii}\,c_i \lambda_i + (\Etilde_0\,R)_{ii}$,
and Cauchy--Schwarz with operator-norm $\norm{\Etilde_0}_{op} \leq E_{op}$
and $\norm{R}_F = O(N^{-1/2})$ from (A1) gives
$|(\Etilde_0\,R)_{ii}| \leq \norm{\Etilde_0}_{op}\,\norm{R}_F = O(N^{-1/2})$.
Plugging these in yields the boxed bound. Full proof:
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} (linear bound) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} (quadratic bound) characterize the same
phenomenon at different levels of refinement. The linear bound is
\emph{model-free}: it holds for any kBM flow with bounded gradients,
regardless of how $G_0$ and $K$ are related. The quadratic bound exploits
the additional structure of NTK initialization (Proposition~\ref{prop:align}).
We use the linear bound as the formal statement of the spectral-bias
phenomenon and the quadratic bound as a sharper prediction within the
NTK regime; experiments support the quadratic prediction
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral}).
\end{remark}

This is a sharper statement than ``the NTK influences training.'' It
says: the network's trajectory is confined, on any bounded time
interval, to a neighborhood of its initial state \emph{in the bottom
eigenspace of $K$}, with the neighborhood size controlled linearly in
$\lambda_i$ in general (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}) and quadratically
under NTK initialization (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}).
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} verifies this prediction numerically and
shows that it holds for both networks and the ODE.

\subsection{Generalization implication}
\label{sec:gen}

Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} has a direct generalization
implication: the network cannot fit triplet structure that lives in
$K$'s bottom eigenspace within any bounded training time. So if the
target geometry has support outside $K$'s top-$r$ eigenspace, the
network's effective hypothesis class on a finite training horizon is
truncated to that subspace.

\begin{corollary}[Effective rank of the hypothesis class]
\label{cor:gen}
Let $P_{>r}$ denote orthogonal projection in $K$'s eigenbasis onto the
eigenspaces with index $> r$. Assume (A1)--(A2) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} together with the
\textbf{(A3) eigenvalue-decay structure on $\Etilde_0$}:
$|(\Etilde_0)_{ij}| \leq c_E\,\lambda_i$ for all $i \neq j$ in the
top-$r$ eigenspace, some constant $c_E > 0$. (A3) is a structural
condition we do not derive from Funk--Hecke alignment; an
empirical probe at $N{=}4000$ on CIFAR-10
(Appendix~\ref{app:etilde-diag}) confirms (A3) holds with $c_E \leq 1$
across the top three quartiles of the spectrum (mean ratio
$\approx 0.04$, $99$th percentile $\leq 0.17$ across $5$ seeds). The
condition degrades only at the spectrum tail
where $\lambda_i \to 0$, but Cor.~\ref{cor:gen}'s effective-rank
truncation is to the top eigenspace, so the regime where (A3) holds
is exactly the regime in which the corollary makes claims.
Then the off-diagonal evolution to leading order in $T$ satisfies
$|\Gtilde_{ij}(T) - \Gtilde_{ij}(0)| \leq 4 c_{\max} T E_{op}\,\lambda_i \lambda_j + O(T^2) + O(T\,N^{-1/2})$,
so combining diagonal and off-diagonal mode-wise bounds yields
\[
\bigl\|P_{>r}\bigl(G(T) - G(0)\bigr)P_{>r}\bigr\|_F
\;\leq\; 4\,c_{\max}\,T\,E_{op}\,\sum_{i > r} \lambda_i^2
\;+\; O(T^2) \;+\; O(T\,N^{-1/2}).
\]
Fix a training horizon $T$ and a tolerance $\delta > 0$, and define
$r(T, \delta)$ as the smallest integer with
$4 c_{\max} T E_{op} \sum_{i > r} \lambda_i^2 < \delta$. Then the projection
of the trajectory onto $K$'s bottom-$(N-r)$ eigenspace is held within
Frobenius distance $\delta$ of its value at initialization, regardless of
how the loss gradient aligns with that subspace.
\end{corollary}

This is an \emph{effective rank} of the hypothesis class: in the NTK
regime, the network can only fit metric structures supported on $K$'s
top-$r(T, \delta)$ eigenspace within training time $T$. In particular,
fitting test-time triplets that live in $K$'s bottom eigenspace requires
either (a) a different kernel (more data, deeper network, different
activation), or (b) the spectral preconditioner of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-precond}. We do not pursue a rate-optimal
generalization bound here---this would require a more involved analysis
of the gradient noise and the loss-landscape geometry---but the
qualitative picture is concrete: the kBM theory gives a precise
formula for which structures are learnable on bounded horizons.

\section{Numerical Illustration}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

We organize the experiments around the theorems above. Synthetic
experiments use unit-norm Gaussian data and triplet loss generated
uniformly at random (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-equiv},
\ref{sec:exp-spectral}, \ref{sec:exp-precond},
\ref{sec:exp-rank-vs-spec}). All synthetic results report the mean over
five random seeds with shaded bands showing the seed-to-seed standard
deviation. Networks use the standard NTK parameterisation, the depth-$L$
NTK is computed via the Jacot recursion, and the kBM ODE is integrated
with RK4 (full hyperparameters in Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). Code,
configurations, and raw multi-seed JSON outputs are included as
supplementary material.

\subsection{Equivalence: network vs.\ kBM ODE}
\label{sec:exp-equiv}

We initialize a fresh ReLU embedder of depth $L \in \{1,2,3\}$ and width
$M=1000$, integrate the kBM ODE from the network's $G_0$, and compare.
Initial Frobenius agreement is at the $10^{-3}$ level and final-epoch
relative Frobenius error remains below $0.22$ across all six $(N, L)$
configurations, growing with depth as the NTK approximation loosens
(full multi-seed trajectories and table in Appendix~\ref{app:equiv-fig}).

\subsection{Spectral implicit bias on synthetic data}
\label{sec:exp-spectral}

We diagonalize the depth-$L$ NTK $K_L = U\Lambda U^\top$ at initialization,
and project the network's Gram matrix into $K_L$'s eigenbasis at every
recorded epoch: $\Gtilde(t) := U^\top G(t) U$. Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}
predicts that the diagonal mode $\Gtilde_{ii}(t) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)$ should
scale with $\lambda_i$, and that low-$\lambda$ modes should be effectively
frozen relative to high-$\lambda$ modes; Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}
sharpens this to a slope-$\leq 2$ power-law upper bound at NTK
initialization, achieved with equality only on the top of the spectrum
where assumption (A1) is approximately diagonal.

Figure~\ref{fig:spectral} confirms this prediction across
$N \in \{30, 100, 1000\}$ and $L \in \{1, 2\}$. Across two-to-three
orders of magnitude in $\lambda_i$, the per-mode displacement is
monotone in the eigenvalue and the power-law fits (in-panel legends)
give slopes ranging from $1.38 \pm 0.001$ ($N{=}1000, L{=}1$) to
$2.19 \pm 0.12$ ($N{=}30, L{=}1$), with the smallest-$N$ point being
the only configuration whose CI extends past 2 --- consistent with
the $O(T\,\lambda\,N^{-1/2})$ residual term in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}, which dominates at small $N$ and
shrinks as $N$ grows (slope $1.32$ at $N{=}3000$ in
Appendix~\ref{app:bound}). The seed-to-seed variation
tightens dramatically as $N$ grows (std $\sim 10^{-3}$ at $N=1000$):
the phenomenon is neither a small-$N$ artifact nor seed noise. Depth
$L$ enters as a robustness control rather than a bias variable, since
the theorem bounds slope by $2$ at every depth without predicting a
monotone $L$-dependence; the slopes vary non-monotonically across
$L$ accordingly. Slopes below $2$ reflect mild deviations from the
diagonal-alignment assumption (A1) at finite width and finite $N$;
extending the sweep to $N \in \{300, 3000\}$ confirms the bound holds
across two further orders of magnitude in $N$
(Appendix~\ref{app:bound}). The same bound is confirmed on
Fashion-MNIST (Appendix~\ref{app:fashion}).

We further verified that the slope of
$|\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)|$ versus $\lambda_i$ is approximately
constant in time (slope $\in [2.00, 2.14]$ from very early to final epoch
on $N=30$, $L=1$), as Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} predicts to leading
order in $T$. The slope of $|\Gtilde_{ii}(T)|$ itself (without subtracting
initialization) drifts from $1.33$ at early time to $1.08$ at convergence,
consistent with $\Gtilde_0[i,i] \approx c_i \lambda_i$ at initialization
(Proposition~\ref{prop:align}) followed by relaxation toward the loss
optimum.

\subsection{A theory-derived correction: the $K^{-1}$ preconditioner}
\label{sec:exp-precond}

The bias originates in the kernel preconditioner $K$ in
$\dot G = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$, so applying $K^{-1}$ to the embedding-side
gradient should cancel it. We implement this as a one-line modification:
replace $dL/d\theta = J^\top\,\mathrm{vec}(dL/dF)$ by
$dL/d\theta = J^\top\,\mathrm{vec}(K^{-1}\,dL/dF)$ before the second
backward pass. In the NTK regime the Gram-matrix dynamics then become
$\dot G = -2(\E G + G\E)$, the un-preconditioned BM flow.

On the synthetic depth-$1$, $N=50$ setup, vanilla has
$s_v = 2.00 \pm 0.11$ and the correction flattens to
$s_p = 0.74 \pm 0.09$ (Figure~\ref{fig:precond}). Total triplet
satisfaction is essentially unchanged ($0.83$ vs.\ $0.81$); the
difference is which spectral modes carry the loss-fitting work, not
how much work is done overall. Whether this redistribution helps
depends on whether the test geometry loads on the suppressed modes
--- tested next in Section~\ref{sec:exp-cifar}.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig_preconditioner.png}
\caption{The $K^{-1}$ correction flattens the spectral bias.
\textbf{Left:} per-mode displacement $|\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)|$
vs.\ kernel eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ at the final epoch (log--log);
slope $0.77$ corrected vs.\ $1.93$ vanilla.
\textbf{Right:} time-resolved trajectories of the top

\subsection{Real-data validation: CIFAR-10 head-only}
\label{sec:exp-cifar}

We repeat on CIFAR-10 using frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18
\citep{he2016deep,deng2009imagenet} penultimate features (PCA to 128 dim,
unit-normalised) and a 2-layer fully-connected ReLU head; 2000 triplets
per seed across $N=400$ images, 10 classes, 5 seeds. The slope-$2$
phenomenon transfers to real image features and the correction's
flattening effect is preserved (Figure~\ref{fig:cifar}).

The slope $2.06 \pm 0.01$ on real features is the closest the empirical

\paragraph{Test-time retrieval (Recall@$K$).}
We split CIFAR-10 into $N=400$ train, $N=200$ test, train on training
triplets, then retrieve test images against the train gallery. Two
triplet sampling regimes test different parts of $K$'s spectrum:
\emph{easy} (random different-class negatives) loads the gradient on
$K$'s top eigenspace --- coarse cluster geometry that the network's
spectral bias already favours; \emph{hard} (nearest different-class
negative in input space) loads the gradient on $K$'s bottom eigenspace
by construction --- the suppressed modes that vanilla training cannot
reach in finite time. The theory predicts no gain in the easy regime
and a gain in the hard regime, and the two halves of
Table~\ref{tab:recall} test these predictions independently.

\subsection{Spectral bias is distinct from rank bias}
\label{sec:exp-rank-vs-spec}

Tracking effective rank of $G(t)$ alongside spectral displacement
$\norm{\Gtilde(t)-\Gtilde(0)}_F$ on the same trajectory ($N=50$),
effective rank stays essentially constant at $\approx 33$ for both
vanilla and corrected variants while spectral displacement differs by
orders of magnitude --- so rank-based and spectral characterisations
respond to different interventions and are not the same phenomenon
(Appendix~\ref{app:rank-vs-spec}; further rank analyses in
Appendices~\ref{app:rank-width}--\ref{app:phase}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A precise, computable hypothesis class for wide-network
metric learning.}
The headline implication of the kBM lens is that the set of metric
geometries a wide network can fit on a bounded training horizon is not
arbitrary: it is precisely the top eigenspace of $K$, with bottom-eigenspace
projections suppressed by $\lambda_i^2$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:gen}). Given any
(data, architecture, training time), a practitioner can compute $K$ once
at initialization, read off its spectrum, and \emph{predict in advance}
which target structures are reachable and which are not. This is a
constructive handle on the implicit hypothesis class. The
rank-minimisation characterisations of
\citet{arora2019implicit,razin2020implicit} and the margin-direction
characterisations of \citet{soudry2018implicit,lyu2019gradient} each
name specific functionals --- nuclear norm of $G$, the max-margin
direction in parameter space --- that gradient descent prefers. We
identify a different object: the data-induced eigenspace of the NTK
along which $G$ is allowed to evolve in finite time. The distinction is
between a preferred functional and a reachable subspace. Our experiments confirm the dissociation:
effective rank of $G(t)$ is essentially constant across training even
when our predicted spectral displacement varies by orders of magnitude
(Appendix~\ref{app:rank-vs-spec}).

\paragraph{Predictive, falsifiable, prescriptive.}
Most implicit-bias results in the literature are \emph{descriptive}:
they identify a tendency and verify it post hoc. The kBM lens delivers
a stronger trifecta. \emph{Predictive}: Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}
specifies a quantitative upper bound (slope $\leq 2$) on the per-mode
displacement power-law;
across $30+$ multi-seed configurations spanning synthetic data,
Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 (head-only and end-to-end CNN) and a ViT
empirical NTK (slope $1.14 \pm 0.07$, Appendix~\ref{app:vit}),
slopes range from $1.10$ to $2.19$ and the slope-$\leq 2$ envelope
holds throughout.
\emph{Falsifiable}: the $K^{-1}$ correction was specified \emph{ahead
of experiment} to help when the test signal lives in $K$'s bottom
eigenspace and to be inert when it lives in the top, and the
$N{=}4000$ CIFAR-10 retrieval data confirm both halves
($+1.82$/$+2.10$/$+2.17$~pp on hard R@$5$/R@$10$/R@$20$, all
Bonferroni $p \leq 0.004$ over $10$ seeds; ties on easy R@$10$ and
R@$20$, Appendix~\ref{app:scaled}). \emph{Prescriptive}: the same
theorems hand a practitioner a one-line code change with a precise
regime of effect, and the same bottom-eigenmode-reach mechanism
yields a downstream adversarial-robustness signature ---
under PGD $\ell_\infty$ $\varepsilon\!=\!8/255$, $K^{-1}$ retains
$5.7\times$ adversarial R@$10$ relative to vanilla
(Appendix~\ref{app:adv}). No benchmark sweeping or hyperparameter
tuning is required to know when the correction will help.

\paragraph{A methodological pattern beyond fully-connected ReLU.}
The four-term contraction underlying
Theorems~\ref{thm:kbm}--\ref{thm:depth} is the key analytical move and
generalises in principle to any feature map whose Gram-Gram NTK admits
the same Gauss--Newton-style factorisation. Fully-connected ReLU is
the only \emph{closed-form} instance we work out here; the empirical
NTK results on a small CNN (Section~\ref{sec:exp-end2end},
Appendix~\ref{app:drift}) and on a ViT (Appendix~\ref{app:vit}) show
that the slope-$\leq 2$ envelope persists empirically when the
preconditioner is taken to be $K^{\mathrm{emp}}$ rather than a
closed-form kernel, but a derivation that recovers a
\emph{closed-form} convolutional or attention NTK with the matching
Gram-Gram expansion is open and is the natural follow-up. Within
either regime --- closed-form FCN or empirical NTK on
finite-width architectures --- the kBM recipe is the same: identify
the kernel preconditioner of the dynamics, diagonalize it, and read
off the reachable subspace from $K$'s spectrum.

\paragraph{Regime of empirical effect.}
The asymmetric retrieval prediction (gain on hard, tie on easy) holds
\emph{robustly} on moderate-class-count benchmarks: CIFAR-10 and
Fashion-MNIST each show three Bonferroni-significant hard-R@$K$ gains
at $10$ seeds, and the $13\times$ scaled $N{=}4000$ replication
strengthens this with paired-$t$ Bonferroni $p\!\leq\!0.004$ on
R@$5$/R@$10$/R@$20$ (Appendix~\ref{app:scaled}). Across a broader
sweep of seven retrieval datasets ranging to 200 fine-grained classes
(Appendix~\ref{app:multidataset}), four datasets show
Bonferroni-significant hard gains; effect sizes attenuate with class
count, and at the highest class counts the strict ``easy ties''
prediction is partially contradicted by a small ($\sim 1$--$3$~pp)
negative effect on easy R@$10$. A consonant finding emerges in the
scaled run at the tightest measures: a small significant negative on
easy R@$1$ and easy R@$5$ at $N{=}4000$ on a low-class-count benchmark.
The most plausible explanation is finite-sample noise amplification:
with few examples per class, the bottom-eigenspace gradient signal is
dominated by noise, which $K^{-1}$ amplifies along with any signal
that is present. The \emph{net asymmetry}
$\Delta_{\text{hard}} > \Delta_{\text{easy}}$ holds on every dataset
we tested. The $K^{-1}$ correction is therefore
most useful when the bottom eigenspace contains discriminating
geometry rather than sampling noise --- an explicit
regime-of-applicability characterisation that distinguishes it from
generic preconditioning heuristics.

\paragraph{Scope, limitations, and open questions.}
kBM relies on the lazy NTK regime
\citep{chizat2020implicit,jacot2018neural}; feature-learning corrections
\citep{mei2018mean} are out of scope, though the spectral-bias
signature persists across training of an end-to-end CNN with the
empirical NTK recomputed at multiple checkpoints
(Appendix~\ref{app:drift}), suggesting the theory is not strictly
limited to the strict-lazy regime in practice.
Theorems~\ref{thm:kbm}--\ref{thm:depth} cover fully-connected ReLU
networks; the CNN result uses the empirical NTK as a proxy and a
closed-form convolutional or attention extension is open. The
empirical NTK signature transfers to a small ViT
(Appendix~\ref{app:vit}, slope $1.14 \pm 0.07$), suggesting the
spectral-bias prediction is architecture-agnostic in practice.
Corollary~\ref{cor:gen} inherits an additional structural condition
(A3) on $\Etilde_0$ that we do not derive from Funk--Hecke alignment
alone but which we verify empirically: at $N{=}4000$ on CIFAR-10 over
$5$ seeds, $|(\Etilde_0)_{ij}|/\lambda_i$ has 99th-percentile $\leq 0.17$
across the top three spectrum quartiles, so $c_E \leq 1$ holds in the
regime where the corollary's top-$r$ truncation operates
(Appendix~\ref{app:etilde-diag}). We do not yield a quantitative
generalisation rate. The $K^{-1}$ correction costs a one-time
$O(N^3)$ kernel inversion and one extra backward pass per step. The asymmetric retrieval prediction is robust
to seed count (10 paired seeds, all primary gains
Bonferroni-significant on CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST), to the choice
of preconditioner exponent (a $K^{-\alpha}$ sweep rules out
generic-preconditioner explanations; Appendix~\ref{app:alpha}), to the
hardness operationalisation (FaceNet-style embedding-space mining
preserves the asymmetry; Appendix~\ref{app:semihard}), and across
seven retrieval benchmarks
(Appendix~\ref{app:multidataset}). Natural follow-ups:
composition with the rank-bias line in deeper factorisations
\citep{arora2019implicit,razin2020implicit}; an implicit spectral
preconditioner via a $K$-dependent learning-rate schedule; a
matrix-valued (anisotropic) NTK extension; and a feature-learning
analogue of the kBM equivalence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We characterized NTK-regime neural metric learning as a
kernel-preconditioned Burer--Monteiro flow on the Gram matrix,
connecting two lines of theoretical work that have been studied in
isolation. The foundational implication is that the diagonal-mode
trajectory of the embedding Gram matrix is constrained by a
quadratic-in-$\lambda$ displacement law
(Theorems~\ref{thm:spectral}--\ref{thm:spectral-refined}). We verified
the slope-$\leq 2$ envelope across $30+$ multi-seed configurations
spanning synthetic data, Fashion-MNIST, frozen-feature CIFAR-10, an
end-to-end CNN trained from scratch, and a ViT empirical NTK
(slope $1.14 \pm 0.07$, Appendix~\ref{app:vit}) --- the prediction
transfers from MLP through CNN to attention-based feature maps --- and
confirmed via an empirical-NTK drift study that the envelope persists
outside the strict-lazy regime (Appendix~\ref{app:drift}). The same
theorems prescribe a one-line $K^{-1}$ correction with an explicit
regime of effect: gains on hard-negative retrieval, ties on easy. We
confirmed this prediction to Bonferroni-corrected significance on
CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST, strengthened it with a $13\times$ scaled
$N{=}4000$ replication (Bonferroni $p\!\leq\!0.004$ on
R@$5$/R@$10$/R@$20$, Appendix~\ref{app:scaled}), documented its
attenuation with class count across five additional benchmarks
(Appendix~\ref{app:multidataset}), and ruled out alternative
explanations via a $K^{-\alpha}$ specificity sweep
(Appendix~\ref{app:alpha}) and FaceNet-style embedding-space mining
(Appendix~\ref{app:semihard}). As a downstream practical-robustness
corroboration of the bottom-eigenmode-reach mechanism, $K^{-1}$ also
retains $5.7\times$ adversarial Recall@$10$ under PGD
$\ell_\infty$ $\varepsilon\!=\!8/255$ (Appendix~\ref{app:adv}). The
kBM viewpoint thereby identifies a \emph{constructive, falsifiable,
and prescriptive} handle on the implicit hypothesis class of
wide-network metric learning --- a combination that distinguishes it
from descriptive rank- and margin-bias characterisations and points to
a methodological pattern applicable beyond metric learning to any
Gram-matrix loss in the NTK feature space.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:kbm}}

\paragraph{Single-layer setup.}
Let $W \in \R^{d \times M}$, $F_{im} = \sigma(x_i^\top w_m)$ where
$w_m$ is the $m$-th column of $W$ and $\sigma(z) = \max(z, 0)$. Let
$s_{im} := \mathbb{1}[x_i^\top w_m > 0]$ be the activation indicator.
Then $\partial F_{im}/\partial W_{kl} = s_{im}\, x_{ik}\, \delta_{ml}$,
where $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta.

The Gram matrix is $G_{ij} = \sum_m F_{im}F_{jm}$. Its parameter-space
gradient is
\[
\frac{\partial G_{ij}}{\partial W_{kl}}
\;=\; \sum_m \!\Bigl(\frac{\partial F_{im}}{\partial W_{kl}} F_{jm}
+ F_{im}\frac{\partial F_{jm}}{\partial W_{kl}}\Bigr)
\;=\; s_{il}\,x_{ik}\,F_{jl} + F_{il}\,s_{jl}\,x_{jk}.
\]

\paragraph{Gram-Gram NTK.}
The Gram-Gram NTK is
$\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} := \sum_{p,q}
(\partial G_{ij}/\partial W_{pq})(\partial G_{kl}/\partial W_{pq})$.
Substituting and contracting over $p, q$ gives a four-term sum
\begin{align*}
\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} \;&=\;
\bigl(x_i^\top x_k\bigr)\,\bigl(\textstyle\sum_q s_{iq}s_{kq}F_{jq}F_{lq}\bigr)
\;+\; \bigl(x_i^\top x_l\bigr)\,\bigl(\textstyle\sum_q s_{iq}s_{lq}F_{jq}F_{kq}\bigr) \\
&\quad+\; \bigl(x_j^\top x_k\bigr)\,\bigl(\textstyle\sum_q s_{jq}s_{kq}F_{iq}F_{lq}\bigr)
\;+\; \bigl(x_j^\top x_l\bigr)\,\bigl(\textstyle\sum_q s_{jq}s_{lq}F_{iq}F_{kq}\bigr).
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Infinite-width limit.}
The $M$ neurons share an iid Gaussian initialization, so per-neuron
random variables $(s_{iq}, s_{kq}, F_{jq}, F_{lq})$ are iid copies
across $q$. The Tensor Programs framework \citep{yang2020tensor}
establishes that the joint expectation
$\E[s_{iq}s_{kq}\,F_{jq}F_{lq}]$ for arbitrary index sets
$\{i, j, k, l\}$ is computable in closed form from the Gaussian
process governing $w_q^\top x$, with cross-correlations between the
indicator pair and the post-activation pair vanishing in the
$M \to \infty$ limit when the relevant input subsets are
asymptotically uncorrelated; together with the Cho--Saul arc-cosine
identities for $\E[s_{iq}s_{kq}]$ and $\E[F_{jq}F_{lq}]$
\citep{cho2009kernel,jacot2018neural}, this yields
\[
\textstyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_q s_{iq}s_{kq}\,F_{jq}F_{lq}
\;\to\; \dot{\Sigma}^{(1)}_{ik}\,\Sigma^{(1)}_{jl} + o(1),
\]
where $\dot{\Sigma}^{(1)}_{ik} = \E[s_{iq}s_{kq}]$ is the
activation-derivative arc-cosine kernel and
$\Sigma^{(1)}_{jl} = \E[F_{jq}F_{lq}]$ is the layer-$1$ activation
kernel. Analogous limits hold for the other three
inner sums. Combining each with its outer $\langle x, x\rangle$ factor and
using the layer-$1$ NTK identity
$\kappa(x_i, x_k) = \Sigma^{(1)}(x_i, x_k) + \dot{\Sigma}^{(1)}(x_i, x_k)\langle x_i, x_k\rangle$
\citep{jacot2018neural}, together with the lazy-regime identification
$G(t) \to \Sigma^{(1)}$ at NTK initialization (and along the trajectory
in the wide limit \citep{lee2019wide}), the four-term sum reduces to
\[
\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} \;=\; K_{ik}G_{jl} + K_{il}G_{jk} + K_{jk}G_{il} + K_{jl}G_{ik},
\]
with $K_{ij} := \kappa(x_i, x_j)$.

\paragraph{Gradient flow on $G$.}
By the chain rule,
$\dot G_{ij} = -\sum_{k,l}\E_{kl}\,\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)}$. Substituting
the four-term expansion, using symmetry of $\E$ and $G$, and contracting
indices yields
$\dot G_{ij} = -2\bigl((K\E G)_{ij} + (G\E K)_{ij}\bigr)$, equivalently
$\dot G = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:depth}}

\paragraph{Recursive NTK at the data.}
For a depth-$L$ ReLU network with width $M$ at each layer and standard NTK
parameterization, \citet{jacot2018neural} prove that the NTK at the data
$x_i, x_j$ converges, as $M \to \infty$, to $\kappa_L(x_i, x_j)$ given
recursively by $\kappa_1 = \Sigma^{(1)} + \dot\Sigma^{(1)}\langle x_i, x_j\rangle$
and $\kappa_\ell = \Sigma^{(\ell)} + \dot\Sigma^{(\ell)}\,\kappa_{\ell-1}$,
where $\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ and $\dot\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ are the layer-$\ell$
arc-cosine kernels of \citet{cho2009kernel}.

\paragraph{Gram-Gram NTK at depth $L$.}
For a depth-$L$ ReLU network in NTK parameterization, write the embedding
as $F_{i\cdot} = \phi_L(x_i; \theta)$ with $\phi_L$ the depth-$L$
representation map. The product structure $G_{ij} = \sum_m F_{im}F_{jm}$
implies, via the same chain-rule argument as in the single-layer case,
\[
\frac{\partial G_{ij}}{\partial \theta_p}
\;=\; \sum_m \!\Bigl(\frac{\partial F_{im}}{\partial \theta_p} F_{jm}
+ F_{im}\frac{\partial F_{jm}}{\partial \theta_p}\Bigr).
\]
Forming $\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} = \sum_p (\partial G_{ij}/\partial \theta_p)(\partial G_{kl}/\partial \theta_p)$
expands into four cross terms, each of the form
$\bigl(\sum_p (\partial F_{im}/\partial\theta_p)(\partial F_{kn}/\partial\theta_p)\bigr) \cdot F_{jm}F_{ln}$
or symmetric permutations thereof. The inner Jacobian--Jacobian sum is
the parameter-space NTK between the embedding outputs $F_{im}$ and
$F_{kn}$. In NTK parameterization with iid Gaussian read-out weights,
the last-layer contribution to the cross-coordinate NTK
($m \neq n$) vanishes in expectation; the earlier-layer contribution
also vanishes because the earlier-layer Jacobians are reweighted by
independent read-out coefficients with mean zero pairwise product. The
joint result --- that the depth-$L$ Gram-Gram NTK converges to a tensor
product of the prediction-side NTK $\kappa_L$ with the Gram matrix $G$,
with cross-coordinate terms vanishing --- is established by the Tensor
Programs framework \citep{yang2020tensor}, which gives the joint NTK
expectation in closed form for any architecture expressible as a
tensor program. Specializing to fully-connected ReLU and using the
Jacot recursion \citep{jacot2018neural,lee2019wide} for the diagonal
$m = n$ piece, the remaining $F_{jm}F_{ln}$ factor sums to $G_{jl}$,
producing the four-term contraction
\[
\Theta^G_{(ij),(kl)} \;=\; (K_L)_{ik}G_{jl} + (K_L)_{il}G_{jk}
+ (K_L)_{jk}G_{il} + (K_L)_{jl}G_{ik}.
\]
The remaining gradient-flow contraction (using $\E^\top = \E$, $G^\top = G$)
is identical to the single-layer case and yields
$\dot G = -2(K_L\E G + G\E K_L)$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}}

\paragraph{Eigenbasis form.}
$K = U\Lambda U^\top$. Conjugating
$\dot G = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$ by $U^\top, U$ on left and right gives
$\dot{\Gtilde} = -2(\Lambda\Etilde\Gtilde + \Gtilde\Etilde\Lambda)$.

\paragraph{Frozen-mode case.}
If $\lambda_i = 0$, then $(\Lambda \Etilde \Gtilde)_{ij} = 0$ for all $j$
(row $i$ of $\Lambda$ is zero) and $(\Gtilde\Etilde\Lambda)_{ji} = 0$ for
all $j$ (column $i$ of $\Lambda$ is zero). The $i$-th row and column of
$\dot{\Gtilde}$ vanish; in particular $\Gtilde_{ij}(t) = \Gtilde_{ij}(0)$
for all $j$ and all $t$.

\paragraph{Bound for small $\lambda_i$.}
Component $(i,j)$ of $\dot{\Gtilde}$ satisfies
\[
|\dot{\Gtilde}_{ij}|
\;=\; 2\,\bigl|\lambda_i (\Etilde\Gtilde)_{ij} + \lambda_j (\Gtilde\Etilde)_{ij}\bigr|
\;\leq\; 2\,(\lambda_i + \lambda_j)\,
\max\bigl(\|\Etilde\Gtilde\|_{op}, \|\Gtilde\Etilde\|_{op}\bigr).
\]
Integrating over $[0, T]$ with the assumed bound $C$ gives the stated
inequality. \qed

\subsection{Gr\"onwall control of the $O(T^2)$ remainder in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}}
\label{app:gronwall}

The boxed bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} is stated ``to
leading order in $T$''. A pointwise $T \to 0^+$ Taylor expansion does
not by itself license integration over a training horizon of
macroscopic length: the $O(T^2)$ remainder constant must be uniform in
$T$. We close this gap with a standard Gr\"onwall argument applied to
the full ODE under (A1)--(A2). Throughout, $\norm{\cdot}_{op}$ is the
spectral norm and $\lambda_{\max} := \max_i \lambda_i$. The hypothesis
$L \in C^2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} entails a local
Hessian bound which we name explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A2$'$)] $\norm{\nabla^2_G L(G)}_{op} \leq L_H$ on
$\{G : \norm{G}_{op} \leq 2\norm{G_0}_{op}\}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[A priori bounds on $\Gtilde$ and $\Etilde$]
\label{lem:gronwall-apriori}
Assume (A1), (A2), (A2$'$). Set $T^\star := \log 2 / (4\lambda_{\max}
E_{op})$. Then for all $s \in [0, T^\star]$,
\begin{align}
\norm{\Gtilde(s)}_{op}
&\;\leq\; \norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}\, e^{4\lambda_{\max} E_{op}\, s}
\;\leq\; 2\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op},
\label{eq:gronwall-G}\\
\norm{\Gtilde(s) - \Gtilde_0}_{op}
&\;\leq\; 8\,\lambda_{\max}\, E_{op}\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}\, s,
\label{eq:gronwall-Gdrift}\\
\norm{\Etilde(s) - \Etilde_0}_{op}
&\;\leq\; 8\, L_H\, \lambda_{\max}\, E_{op}\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}\, s.
\label{eq:gronwall-E}
\end{align}
Each bound has constants \emph{independent of $T$}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conjugating $\dot G = -2(K\E G + G\E K)$ by $U^\top, U$ gives
$\dot{\Gtilde} = -2(\Lambda \Etilde \Gtilde + \Gtilde \Etilde \Lambda)$,
hence by submultiplicativity and (A2),
$\norm{\dot{\Gtilde}(s)}_{op} \leq 4\,\lambda_{\max}\,E_{op}\,
\norm{\Gtilde(s)}_{op}$.
Since $s \mapsto \norm{\Gtilde(s)}_{op}$ is locally Lipschitz with
a.e.\ derivative bounded by $\norm{\dot{\Gtilde}(s)}_{op}$
\citep[Ch.~III]{hartman2002ordinary}, Gr\"onwall yields
\eqref{eq:gronwall-G}; the choice of $T^\star$ ensures the doubling
bound. Integrating the $\dot{\Gtilde}$ bound on $[0, s]$ using
$\norm{\Gtilde}_{op}\leq 2\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}$ gives
\eqref{eq:gronwall-Gdrift}. For \eqref{eq:gronwall-E}, $L \in C^2$
gives $\dot{\Etilde}(s) = U^\top \nabla^2_G L(G(s))[\dot G(s)] U$, so
(A2$'$) and \eqref{eq:gronwall-G} yield
$\norm{\dot{\Etilde}(s)}_{op} \leq 8\,L_H\,\lambda_{\max}\,E_{op}\,
\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}$; integrate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform $O(T^2)$ remainder]
\label{lem:gronwall-remainder}
Define $f_i(s) := (\Etilde(s)\,\Gtilde(s))_{ii}$. Under the hypotheses
of Lemma~\ref{lem:gronwall-apriori}, for all $T \in [0, T^\star]$,
\[
\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)
\;=\; -\,4\,T\,\lambda_i\, f_i(0)
\;-\; 4\,\lambda_i \int_0^T \bigl[\,f_i(s) - f_i(0)\,\bigr]\,ds,
\]
and the integrated remainder is bounded by
\[
\Bigl|\,4\,\lambda_i \int_0^T [\,f_i(s) - f_i(0)\,]\,ds\,\Bigr|
\;\leq\; C_{\mathrm{rem}}\,\lambda_i\,\lambda_{\max}\,T^{\,2},
\]
with $C_{\mathrm{rem}} := 16\, E_{op}\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}\,
(E_{op} + L_H\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op})$ independent of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integral identity is the fundamental theorem of calculus on
$\dot{\Gtilde}_{ii}(s) = -4\lambda_i f_i(s)$. For any $M$,
$|M_{ii}|\leq\norm{M}_{op}$, so adding/subtracting $\Etilde_0\Gtilde(s)$
gives
$\norm{\Etilde(s)\Gtilde(s) - \Etilde_0\Gtilde_0}_{op} \leq
\norm{\Etilde(s)}_{op}\,\norm{\Gtilde(s) - \Gtilde_0}_{op}
+ \norm{\Etilde(s) - \Etilde_0}_{op}\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}$.
By (A2), \eqref{eq:gronwall-Gdrift}, \eqref{eq:gronwall-E},
$|f_i(s) - f_i(0)| \leq 8\,E_{op}\,\lambda_{\max}\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op}\,
(E_{op} + L_H\,\norm{\Gtilde_0}_{op})\, s$. Multiplying by $4\lambda_i$
and integrating $\int_0^T s\,ds = T^2/2$ gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} holds over the
integrated horizon]
\label{cor:gronwall}
For all $T \in [0, T^\star]$,
\[
\bigl|\Gtilde_{ii}(T) - \Gtilde_{ii}(0)\bigr|
\;\leq\; 4\,c_{\max}\,T\,E_{op}\,\lambda_i^{\,2}
\;+\; 4\,T\,\lambda_i\,E_{op}\,\norm{R}_F
\;+\; C_{\mathrm{rem}}\,\lambda_i\,\lambda_{\max}\, T^{\,2},
\]
where $R = \Gtilde_0 - \diag(\Gtilde_0)$ has $\norm{R}_F = O(N^{-1/2})$
by~(A1). The slope-$\leq 2$ envelope therefore persists over $[0, T^\star]$,
not merely as a local $T \to 0^+$ statement.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemma~\ref{lem:gronwall-remainder} with the leading-order
decomposition $f_i(0) = (\Etilde_0)_{ii}\,c_i \lambda_i +
(\Etilde_0\,R)_{ii}$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}: the first term contributes
$\leq 4\,c_{\max}\,T\,E_{op}\,\lambda_i^2$; the second is bounded by
$4\,T\,\lambda_i\,E_{op}\,\norm{R}_F$ via Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remark on $T^\star$.}
The choice $T^\star = \log 2 / (4\lambda_{\max} E_{op})$ is conservative
(it allows $\norm{\Gtilde}_{op}$ to at most double), on which interval
(A2) is self-consistent with the $\Etilde$-drift estimate
\eqref{eq:gronwall-E}. Larger horizons are admissible whenever an a
priori bound on $\norm{\Gtilde(s)}_{op}$ is available from problem
structure (e.g.\ the bounded-active-set argument used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}); only $C_{\mathrm{rem}}$ changes. The
qualitative slope-$\leq 2$ envelope, validated in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral}, is robust to this choice.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:align}}
\label{app:prop-align}

We give the rigorous Funk--Hecke argument. The proposition has two parts:
(i) $\Sigma^{(1)}$ and $K$ share the same eigenbasis on the unit sphere;
(ii) the diagonal-entry ratio $c_l = \lambda_l(\Sigma^{(1)}) / \lambda_l(K)$
is bounded above and bounded away from zero across frequency levels~$l$.

\paragraph{Funk--Hecke decomposition.}
Let $f \colon [-1, 1] \to \R$ be continuous and let
$T_f \colon L^2(S^{d-1}) \to L^2(S^{d-1})$ be the integral operator
$T_f g(x) = \int_{S^{d-1}} f(\langle x, y\rangle) g(y)\,d\sigma(y)$,
where $d\sigma$ is the uniform probability measure on $S^{d-1}$. The
Funk--Hecke theorem \citep[Theorem 2.22]{atkinson2012spherical} states that
$T_f$ is diagonalized by the spherical harmonics $\{Y_{l,k}\}$
of degrees $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, with eigenvalue
\[
\lambda_l(f)
\;=\; \omega_{d-2} \int_{-1}^1 f(t)\,\frac{P_l^{(d)}(t)}{P_l^{(d)}(1)}\,
(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\,dt,
\]
where $P_l^{(d)}$ is the Gegenbauer (ultraspherical) polynomial of degree
$l$ in dimension $d$ and $\omega_{d-2}$ is the surface area of $S^{d-2}$.

\paragraph{Both kernels are functions of $\langle x, y\rangle$.}
For the layer-1 ReLU arc-cosine kernel,
$\Sigma^{(1)}(x, y) = \kappa_1(\langle x, y\rangle)$ for unit-norm
$x, y$, where $\kappa_1(t) = \tfrac{1}{\pi}(\sqrt{1-t^2} + (\pi - \arccos t) t)$
\citep{cho2009kernel}. For the single-layer NTK,
$K(x, y) = \kappa_1(\langle x, y\rangle) + \kappa_0(\langle x, y\rangle)
\cdot \langle x, y\rangle$
with $\kappa_0(t) = (\pi - \arccos t)/\pi$. Both are scalar functions of
$\langle x, y\rangle$, so by Funk--Hecke they diagonalize in the
spherical-harmonic basis with eigenvalues $\lambda_l(\kappa_1)$ and
$\lambda_l(\kappa_1 + \kappa_0 \cdot t)$ respectively. This proves~(i).

\paragraph{Bounded ratio across $l$.}
Define $c_l := \lambda_l(\kappa_1) / \lambda_l(\kappa_1 + \kappa_0 \cdot t)$.
By the Funk--Hecke formula and linearity,
$\lambda_l(\kappa_1 + \kappa_0 \cdot t)
= \lambda_l(\kappa_1) + \lambda_l(\kappa_0 \cdot t)$, so
\[
c_l \;=\; \frac{\lambda_l(\kappa_1)}{\lambda_l(\kappa_1) + \lambda_l(\kappa_0 \cdot t)}
\;\in\; (0, 1).
\]
Both $\lambda_l(\kappa_1)$ and $\lambda_l(\kappa_0 \cdot t)$ are strictly
positive for all $l$: the kernels $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_0 \cdot t$ are
strictly positive-definite on $S^{d-1}$ for $d \geq 2$ \citep{bach2017breaking}.
This gives $c_l \in (0, 1)$ for all $l$.

For the lower bound, on any finite range $l \in \{0, 1, \ldots, L_{\max}\}$
both $\lambda_l(\kappa_1)$ and $\lambda_l(\kappa_0 \cdot t)$ are strictly
positive (by the strict positive-definiteness of $\kappa_1$ and
$\kappa_0 \cdot t$ on $S^{d-1}$ for $d \geq 2$, established by
\citet{bach2017breaking}), so $c_l \geq c_{\min}(L_{\max}) > 0$ uniformly
on that range. For finite $N$, the empirical eigenstructure of $K^{(N)}$
is supported on at most $N$ harmonic modes, so the relevant range
$L_{\max}$ is finite and a uniform $c_{\min} > 0$ exists; we use this
$c_{\min}$ throughout. (A uniform-in-$l$ lower bound as $l \to \infty$
would require explicit asymptotic rates for both kernels and is not
needed for the finite-$N$ statement.) The trivial upper bound is
$c_{\max} \leq 1$.

\paragraph{From population to empirical.}
For $N$ data points sampled iid from the uniform measure on $S^{d-1}$,
the empirical kernel matrix $K^{(N)}_{ij} = K(x_i, x_j)$ satisfies, with
high probability, $\norm{K^{(N)} - K}_{op} = O(N^{-1/2})$ by standard
matrix-Bernstein concentration \citep{tropp2015introduction}. The same
applies to $\Sigma^{(1),(N)}$. By the Davis--Kahan eigenvector perturbation
theorem (see e.g.\ \citealp{stewart1990matrix}), the eigenvectors of the
empirical kernels converge to projections of spherical harmonics with
errors that are bounded in operator norm by a multiple of the same
$O(N^{-1/2})$ rate, divided by the relevant spectral gap. The alignment
claim of the proposition therefore holds up to $O(N^{-1/2})$ deviations
at finite $N$.

\paragraph{Combining for the diagonal-entry claim.}
Writing the population kernels in their shared eigenbasis $U_\infty$
(spherical harmonics) and using $\Sigma^{(1)} = K \cdot \mathrm{diag}(c_l)$
in that basis, the empirical $\Gtilde_0[i,i]$ at finite $N$ satisfies
$\Gtilde_0[i,i] = c_{l(i)} \lambda_i + O(N^{-1/2}, M^{-1/2})$ where
$l(i)$ is the spherical-harmonic frequency level of the $i$-th
empirical eigenvector. With $c_l \in [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$ this proves
the proposition. \qed

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}

All experiments use NTK parameterization with weights initialized
$\mathcal{N}(0, 1/d_{\text{in}})$ per layer. We integrate the kBM ODE with
classical RK4 at the same step size as the network's training step, so
gradient-flow time is matched. The depth-$L$ NTK is computed via the
Cho--Saul arc-cosine recursion of \citet{cho2009kernel,jacot2018neural}.

For the end-to-end CIFAR-10 experiment we train a 3-block CNN
($32$-$32$-$64$ channels, $32$-dim projection head, $\sim$104k params) on
$N=100$ images with triplet loss. The empirical NTK at initialization is
computed by per-output-feature backward passes. The preconditioned variant
uses $(K^{\text{emp}} + \varepsilon I)^{-1}$ with $\varepsilon = 10^{-2}$.

Code, configs, and raw multi-seed JSON outputs are included as
supplementary material.

\section{Preconditioner specificity: a $K^{-\alpha}$ sweep}
\label{app:alpha}

A natural reviewer concern is whether \emph{any} reasonable
preconditioner --- not $K^{-1}$ specifically --- would flatten the
spectral slope. To rule this out we sweep the preconditioner exponent
$\alpha$ in $K^{-\alpha}$ on the synthetic depth-$1$, $N{=}50$ setup
(5 seeds, identical configuration to Section~\ref{sec:exp-precond}
otherwise). $\alpha = 0$ is vanilla training; $\alpha = 1$ is the
canonical kBM correction; $\alpha > 1$ over-corrects.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{fig_alpha_sweep.png}
\caption{Slope of per-mode displacement vs.\ $\lambda_i$ as a function

$2.08 \to 0.70$ as $\alpha$ goes $0 \to 0.5$, then $\to 1.02$ at

\label{fig:alpha}
\end{figure}

The fitted slopes are
$2.08 \pm 0.11$ at $\alpha=0$,
$0.70 \pm 0.13$ at $\alpha=0.5$,
$0.91 \pm 0.04$ at $\alpha=1.0$,
$1.01 \pm 0.02$ at $\alpha=1.5$, and
$1.02 \pm 0.02$ at $\alpha=2.0$. The slope is non-monotone in $\alpha$:

acts as a preconditioner of the un-preconditioned BM flow. Both
qualitative features are predicted by the kBM equation and rule out
the alternative hypothesis that any preconditioner with comparable
conditioning would produce the same slope flattening.

\section{Bound verification: slope vs $N$}
\label{app:bound}

Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}'s upper bound (empirical slope
$\leq 2$) is a leading-order, asymptotic-in-$N$ statement, with
finite-$N$ residual $O(N^{-1/2})$ from the diagonal-alignment
assumption (A1). To verify the bound holds with growing $N$ and
diagnose the small-$N$ regime, we sweep
$N \in \{30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000\}$ at $L = 1$ on the synthetic
setup of Section~\ref{sec:exp-spectral} ($5$ seeds per $N$, otherwise
identical configuration).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{fig_bound_check.png}
\caption{Empirical slope of per-mode displacement vs.\ kernel eigenvalue
as a function of $N$. Mean $\pm$ std over 5 seeds. The slope settles
below the bound at $N \geq 100$ and continues to decrease toward
$\approx 1.32$ as $N$ grows. The $N = 30$ point ($2.19 \pm 0.12$) is
consistent with the finite-$N$ residual in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} together with small-sample
regression noise on a $30$-mode log-log fit, and is absorbed as $N$
grows by orders of magnitude.}
\label{fig:bound}
\end{figure}

The empirical slope drops sharply from $2.188 \pm 0.120$ at $N = 30$ to
$1.489 \pm 0.027$ at $N = 100$, $1.379 \pm 0.001$ at $N = 300$,
$1.380 \pm 0.001$ at $N = 1000$, and $1.322 \pm 0.0002$ at $N = 3000$
--- the slope is bounded above by $2$ at all $N \geq 100$ and continues
to drop slightly as $N$ grows. Two effects drive this:

\emph{(i) Finite-$N$ residual at small $N$.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined} contains an explicit
$O(T \lambda_i N^{-1/2})$ residual. At $N = 30$ this term is
non-negligible relative to the leading $T \lambda_i^2$ contribution,
and combined with small-sample regression noise on a $30$-mode
log-log fit, it inflates the empirical slope estimate to
$2.188 \pm 0.120$ --- consistent with the bound up to the residual
scale. The excess dissolves by $N = 100$.

\emph{(ii) OLS slope is a top-vs-bottom-spectrum mixture.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}'s bound is a leading-order
\emph{per-mode} statement: top-spectrum modes have displacement
$\sim \lambda_i^2$ (slope $2$), while bottom-spectrum modes are
governed by Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}'s linear $O(T(\lambda_i+\lambda_j))$
bound (slope $1$). The OLS slope from a single power-law fit across the
full spectrum is therefore a weighted average of these two regimes,
between $1$ and $2$. As $N$ grows, the eigenvalue spectrum of the
arc-cosine NTK acquires more bottom-tail modes, pulling the average
slope downward from $\approx 1.49$ at $N = 100$ toward $\approx 1.32$
at $N = 3000$. This is consistent with both theorems, and reflects the
mixture of regimes rather than a violation of either bound.

The bound therefore holds in the regime where it is formally claimed;
what looked like a violation at $N = 30$ is the predicted finite-$N$
correction, and the slow downward drift at large $N$ reflects the
top-vs-bottom-spectrum OLS mixture rather than a contradiction with
theory.

\paragraph{Why the bound is informative even though it is not tight.}
A reasonable objection is that an empirical slope settling at $1.32$ at
$N = 3000$ does not visibly saturate a $\leq 2$ bound, raising the
question of whether the bound is informative. Two facts make the bound
load-bearing despite not being saturated. First, the bound is the
\emph{envelope} that constrains all modes simultaneously: at any
single mode $i$ with sufficiently large $\lambda_i$, displacement
scales as $\lambda_i^2$ (the leading-order term dominates), and
removing that term collapses the predicted regime-of-effect of the
$K^{-1}$ correction to a generic-preconditioner story --- which the
$K^{-\alpha}$ sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha}) rules out empirically.
Second, the OLS mixture argument decomposes the empirical slope into
contributions from the two formally proved bounds (slope-1 from
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} and slope-2 from
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-refined}), so the empirical $\sim 1.3$
slope is the direct prediction of the theory once one accounts for the
mixture. Strictly tighter (matching) lower bounds on per-mode
displacement are open work; we do not claim them.

with low-margin directions, $K^{-1}$-trained retrieval should exhibit a
larger embedding-space \emph{robustness margin}. We test this directly:
on $N_{\mathrm{train}}=400$ + $N_{\mathrm{test}}=200$ CIFAR-10 images
(10 classes, ResNet-18 features PCA-projected to $d=128$), we train a
depth-$2$ width-$1024$ metric head with vanilla and $K^{-1}$-corrected
gradient flow. We then attack each test image with $10$-step
$\ell_\infty$ PGD ($\varepsilon = 8/255$, $\alpha = 2/255$), pushing it
away from its own-class centroid in embedding space, and re-evaluate
Recall@$K$ on the perturbed test set.

\section{Probing the (A3) structural assumption of Cor.~\ref{cor:gen}}
\label{app:etilde-diag}

Cor.~\ref{cor:gen}'s off-diagonal $\lambda_i\lambda_j$ scaling depends
on a structural condition (A3): $|(\Etilde_0)_{ij}| \leq c_E\,\lambda_i$
for off-diagonal $(i,j)$ in the top eigenspace. We probe this directly.
For each of $5$ seeds, we build $Z\in\R^{N\times d}$ via the same
scaled-CIFAR-10 protocol ($N{=}4000$, ResNet-50 features
PCA-projected to $d{=}256$ and unit-normalised), form the depth-$2$
NTK $K = U\Lambda U^\top$, compute
$\E_0 = \partial L/\partial G$ at initialization for the standard
hard-mining hinge triplet loss with $12{,}000$ triplets, project into
$K$'s eigenbasis as $\Etilde_0 = U^\top \E_0 U$, and report the
per-entry ratio $|(\Etilde_0)_{ij}|/\lambda_i$ for off-diagonal
$(i,j)$, broken into eigenvalue quartiles of the spectrum.

bounded. Statistics are reported globally and broken into spectrum

$c_E \leq 1$ across the top three quartiles ($Q_0$--$Q_2$) on every
seed, with mean ratio $\approx 0.04$ and 99th percentile

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Across the top three spectrum quartiles ($Q_0$--$Q_2$, comprising the
top $75\%$ of eigenvalues), the maximum ratio
$|(\Etilde_0)_{ij}|/\lambda_i$ stays below $1$ on every seed, and the
99th-percentile ratio is at most $0.17$. The mean ratio
$\approx 0.04$ is essentially constant across seeds and across
quartiles $Q_0$--$Q_2$. This is empirical evidence that (A3) holds
with $c_E \leq 1$ in the regime Cor.~\ref{cor:gen}'s effective-rank
truncation $P_{>r}$ targets. The bottom-quartile $Q_3$ blow-up (max
ratios $2.3$--$5.2$) is a finite-$N$ artefact at $\lambda_i \to 0$:
small absolute off-diagonal entries divided by tiny $\lambda_i$
produce a large ratio, but the corresponding modes are precisely those
that Cor.~\ref{cor:gen} \emph{drops} via the top-$r$ projection.

\section{Phase-transition under nuclear-norm regularization}
\label{app:phase}

Adding $\lambda\norm{G}_*$ to the objective drives the rank down at large

width 200, 5000 epochs) starting near $\lambda \approx 1$ and reaching
minimum effective rank around $\lambda \approx 20$. The location is
seed-dependent at the tens-of-percent level, so we report this as an
